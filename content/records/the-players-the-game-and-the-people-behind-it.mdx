# MapleStory, Inkwell and GM authority
There's been quite a bit of drama surrounding MapleStory Classic recently, with Inkwell finding himself at the centre of it.
A clip surfaced showing him summoning highly sought-after field bosses, including Mushmom and King Slime, inside his Henesys house while players were visiting. Considering how long these bosses take to respawn and how contested their spawns are, people naturally started questioning the situation.

Things escalated when players began discussing the footage on Reddit and using megaphones in-game to voice their frustrations. One player who publicly criticized the situation was reportedly killed by a summoned boss and subsequently banned.
There were also reports of Inkwell warning players over their comments, which certainly didn't help matters.

Nexon has since addressed the situation, explaining that the account restrictions were caused by an incorrect detection involving a dummy monster in the housing system. According to their statement, multiple players were affected and the restrictions were lifted.
They also clarified that the boss summons were part of their ongoing GM events, rather than something intended to benefit a particular player.

Regardless of the technical explanation, I think there's a bigger discussion to be had here about GM authority, transparency and how the people running a game interact with its community.
When you're in a position of authority, you have to be particularly careful about how you handle criticism. Even the perception of favoritism or unequal treatment can damage the trust between players and the people managing their game.

# GTA VI - Expectations versus reality
I'm not particularly interested in playing GTA VI until it eventually makes its way to PC. Surprisingly, I don't feel much FOMO either. I think I've gradually grown out of GTA, at least to some extent.
I completely skipped the GTA V RP scene, despite having watched hundreds of hours of other people playing it. It was genuinely hilarious, and I had a great time watching it, but I never felt the need to participate myself.

What I do find interesting is the reception GTA VI has received. The sheer number of perfect scores is something else. I find myself questioning whether gaming awards and review culture are becoming increasingly disconnected from the average player.
The Game Awards, in particular, feels increasingly like a commercial marketplace rather than the celebration of games it once presented itself as. That's not to say it has completely abandoned its original purpose, but the direction it seems to be heading in is something I find increasingly difficult to relate to. And opinions surrounding GTA VI aren't exactly unanimous either. Some people dislike how restrictive certain aspects of the gameplay feel, others think it resembles Red Dead Redemption 2 more than the GTA they expected, and there are also the usual technical complaints.

Personally, I think these criticisms need to be separated.

Technical issues are something Rockstar can work on and hopefully resolve through patches. Gameplay, storytelling and creative direction are an entirely different matter. Perhaps this is simply the direction Rockstar wants to take the franchise. If that's the case, **players will either have to get used to it or accept that the series might no longer be for them**.

**That's okay. Not every direction a developer takes has to appeal to everyone.**

I don't particularly agree with the direction GTA VI seems to be heading in, but that has absolutely nothing to do with the usual arguments surrounding wokeism or whatever the current outrage happens to be. I simply don't like the direction. Sometimes that's all there is to it.

**Not every disagreement needs to become an ideological argument, and not every game needs to cater to our expectations.**
