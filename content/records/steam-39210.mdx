:::note 22 / 06 / 2017- The first Hiatus
I eventually took a long break from FFXIV, leaving Eorzea behind for a while. Most of that journey had been spent on my own, and perhaps that made the experience of traveling through Ishgard as a Dark Knight feel even more fitting.

But even after stepping away, Heavensward remained firmly in my memories. The atmosphere, the characters, the battles, and especially the losses had left their mark.

**ARR made me feel at home in Eorzea. Heavensward made me understand what it meant to fight for it.**
:::

:::note 25 / 02 / 2017 - Heavensward
If A Realm Reborn was about discovering a new world, Heavensward was about realizing just how dark that world could become.

I still remember the feeling of arriving in Ishgard for the first time. After spending so much time exploring ARR's colorful forests, deserts, and lively cities, suddenly finding myself surrounded by enormous Gothic architecture, towering pillars, snow, and an atmosphere of constant hostility was incredible.

It almost felt like stepping into Berserk.

I loved it immediately. Ishgard was cold, gloomy, intimidating, and completely different from the Eorzea I'd grown accustomed to. There was something fascinating about its oppressive atmosphere, and the Pillars in particular left me genuinely in awe.

The story complemented that change in tone beautifully. The Dragonsong War, the conflict between dragons and mankind, and the gradually unfolding truth behind Ishgard's history made everything feel more serious. There was a greater sense of consequence to what was happening, and the journey felt less like an ordinary adventure and more like becoming involved in something much larger than yourself.

Haurchefant's death was especially difficult.

Up until that point, I had grown comfortable with the idea of being the Warrior of Light, the hero who arrives when things go wrong and makes everything better. Then suddenly, someone was sacrificing their life to protect *me*.

I remember feeling that it should have been the other way around. I was supposed to protect them, not stand there while they gave their lives for mine.

That moment added a different kind of weight to the journey. Heavensward wasn't afraid to remind me that being a hero doesn't mean being able to save everyone.

The expansion also introduced Dark Knight, a job I was naturally drawn to as a Berserk fan. I didn't switch immediately, spending some more time with Black Mage and even experimenting with Dragoon because of the Dragonsong War storyline. Dragoon didn't quite click with me back then, although it's become considerably more enjoyable with its later additions.

Eventually, though, Dark Knight won me over. I loved the idea of being the party's tank, drawing all the attention and protecting everyone while playing this dark, edgy swordsman wielding an enormous greatsword. Its surprisingly intense job questline only made me appreciate it more, and Dark Knight eventually became my main job all the way to level 80.

It also fit the atmosphere of Heavensward perfectly. There's something about traveling through those cold, hostile environments as a lone Dark Knight that made the adventure feel particularly memorable.

And then there's the actual content.

Alexander, the Void Ark raids, Nidhogg, King Thordan... Heavensward delivered some absolutely fantastic encounters. The music, especially *Dragonsong*, elevated so many moments, and the increasing difficulty of dungeons and raids made the gameplay feel like a natural progression from ARR.

Of course, remembering all these highlights can make the expansion sound almost perfect. But actually playing through it was a different experience.

There were plenty of slower stretches between those incredible moments, and not every part of the journey carried the same excitement. Looking back, it's easy to remember the spectacular encounters and emotional scenes while forgetting how much quieter progression happened between them.

Still, the addition of flying mounts was wonderful. Being able to explore familiar environments from an entirely new perspective added another layer to the sense of adventure.

By the end, Heavensward had completely won me over. I was invested in its characters, its world, and the direction the story was taking.

But finishing it also left me with a lingering sadness.

After everything that had happened, moving forward felt almost like leaving something behind while still grieving it. The adventure had to continue, but that didn't mean I could simply forget the people who hadn't made it.
:::

:::note 22 / 01 / 2017 - A Realm Reborn
A Realm Reborn holds a special place in my memories. Not necessarily because it had the best story, the most exciting encounters, or the most impressive environments, but because it was where everything began. There's a particular kind of nostalgia I associate with ARR that I don't think any other expansion could quite replicate.

My first introduction to Eorzea was actually during the beta, when I started my adventure in Gridania. I remember loving the Black Shroud almost immediately. The forests, the atmosphere, the little dungeons, and the feeling of exploring a world I knew practically nothing about. Everything was unfamiliar, and that was part of the charm.

When I eventually returned to play the full game, I started fresh as a Hyur in Ul'dah, beginning as a Thaumaturge before becoming a Black Mage. And there's one memory from those early days that has stayed with me more vividly than almost anything else.

I remember being outside Ul'dah at night, listening to its nighttime theme, *Sultana Dreaming*, and simply looking up at the sky for several minutes. I wasn't doing quests, fighting monsters, or progressing toward anything. I was just standing there, taking in the atmosphere.

It's difficult to explain exactly what that music made me feel. The closest comparison I can think of is finally letting out a long breath after a stressful day. There's something incredibly peaceful and comforting about it, and I remember being completely enamoured with the moment.

That's what I associate with ARR more than anything else: the simple pleasure of existing in Eorzea.

I spent a lot of time exploring Thanalan and the Black Shroud, which remain two of my favorite regions from the original game. The music, the environments, the changing atmosphere between day and night... I have so many memories of wandering around, stopping to admire the scenery, or simply taking screenshots.

In some ways, those experiences remind me of early MapleStory. Back when everything felt enormous, getting lost was part of the adventure, and you didn't necessarily need a particular objective to enjoy yourself. Sometimes exploring an unfamiliar area was rewarding enough.

The story initially complements that feeling quite well. ARR welcomes you into its world, gradually introduces its people and conflicts, and gives you time to become comfortable with your surroundings. It's charming, almost cozy, particularly during those early hours when you're still just another adventurer trying to find your place.

But as the narrative progresses, that welcoming atmosphere starts to change. You become involved with the Scions, confront increasingly dangerous threats, and begin to understand that Eorzea is far more complicated than it first appeared.

I appreciate that contrast. The world that initially felt so comforting gradually reveals a much darker side, and the story becomes increasingly serious as your responsibilities grow.

That said, the pacing can be genuinely exhausting.

The early introduction to Eorzea was lovely, but the Garlean Empire storyline and especially the Seventh Astral Era quests felt heavy. Not necessarily bad, but there's an enormous amount of information, dialogue, and story progression to absorb, and it can become tiring.

I can absolutely understand why experienced players often recommend that newcomers take their time with ARR. Trying to rush through everything just to reach the later expansions can make an already lengthy experience feel considerably more overwhelming.

And I certainly had moments where I wished I could just finish the story and move on.

Thankfully, ARR also has some fantastic encounters that help break up that slower progression.

I still remember fighting Ifrit for the first time. There was something exciting about finally confronting one of Final Fantasy's iconic summons in an MMO setting. Ultima Weapon was another particularly cool encounter, and a fitting centerpiece for the original storyline's conclusion.

But Shiva deserves a special mention.

That moment when she snaps her fingers and the music suddenly changes mid-fight is just absurdly good. It's such a memorable piece of presentation, the kind of small detail that turns an already enjoyable encounter into something you remember years later. FFXIV has always been particularly good at combining music and boss encounters, and Shiva was an early example of just how effective that could be.

Looking back, I think ARR's greatest strength is also something that makes it difficult to judge purely on its own.

It's an enormous foundation for everything that follows. It introduces the world, the people, the conflicts, and so many elements that become relevant much later. Sometimes that foundation comes at the expense of pacing, but it also makes the journey feel more substantial.

And when I finally reached the end, I experienced a strange mixture of emotions.

I was happy, of course. I had finished a long journey, and Heavensward was waiting with new places to explore, new jobs to try, and more dungeons, trials, and raids to experience. I was genuinely looking forward to what came next.

But I also felt unexpectedly melancholic.

After spending so much time wishing I could get through some of the slower sections, I suddenly found myself wishing I could go back.

Back to being that inexperienced Thaumaturge wandering around Thanalan, discovering unfamiliar places, getting lost, and having absolutely no idea how much the world was going to open up.

I think that's a feeling many long-running games struggle to recreate. As you become more experienced, you gain knowledge, confidence, and a better understanding of everything around you. But you also lose a little of that initial wonder, when even the simplest things could feel special.

ARR gave me that feeling in abundance.

It's far from a flawless experience, and I wouldn't pretend that every hour of its story was equally enjoyable. Some portions are slow, some are unnecessarily demanding of your patience, and I completely understand why newcomers occasionally struggle with it.

But I also think there's something wonderful about how much time it gives you to become familiar with Eorzea before sending you into everything that comes afterward.

For me, A Realm Reborn is more than just the introduction to Final Fantasy XIV. It's a collection of little memories that have stayed with me for years. The forests of Gridania, the deserts of Thanalan, the music of Ul'dah at night, my first encounters with the Primals, and the simple joy of exploring somewhere new.

I was excited to leave for Heavensward, but part of me wanted to remain in that familiar world just a little longer.

**Sometimes I wish I could experience it all again, knowing absolutely nothing.**
